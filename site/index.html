Privacy

Safe to open. Private by design.

Why macOS warns you the first time

Ember is free and made independently, so it isn't notarized by Apple (that needs a paid developer account). macOS will say it can't verify the developer. That's expected, and the steps to open it are under Get started. Ember's code is public, so anyone can check exactly what it does.

Why it asks for permissions

Some features need macOS's permission: Calendar and Reminders to show what's next, and Bluetooth to show your AirPods' battery. Ember asks for each permission only when you turn on the feature that needs it, during setup or later in Settings. Say no, and only that feature stays off. You can change your mind any time in System Settings → Privacy & Security.

What stays on your Mac: everything

No account, no analytics, no tracking, no servers. Your calendar, devices and files are read on your Mac, shown in the notch, and never sent anywhere. Ember only goes online to check GitHub for updates.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What is Ember?

A free Mac app that turns the notch into a small, living status area. At rest it's a black pill that blends in. When something happens, like plugging in your charger or a timer running, it glows and shows it. Hover, and it opens into controls.

Which Macs does it work on?

MacBooks with a notch (MacBook Pro 14" and 16" from 2021, MacBook Air from 2022) running macOS 15 Sequoia or later. External displays and Macs without a notch aren't supported yet.

How do I install it?

Ember isn't out yet. When it is, you'll download it here, drag it into Applications, and follow the one-time steps under "First time you open it?".

Does it cost anything?

No. Ember is free and open source under the GPL-3.0 licence: no ads, no account, no in-app purchases.

Will it slow down my Mac or drain the battery?

It's built to stay light: it only redraws when something changes. We'll measure it on real Macs before the first release.

How does it update?

Ember checks GitHub for new versions and asks before installing one. That check is the only time it goes online.

Opened for the first time?

macOS blocks apps that aren't notarized the first time. Open Ember once, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to Ember. You only do this once.
