Ember

Your notch, glowing.

A free, open-source Dynamic Island for your MacBook.

What it does

At rest, Ember is a black pill that blends into the notch. When something happens, it glows. Hover, and it opens.

Now Playing Planned

Album art, play and pause, skip and a scrubber, right below the notch.

Battery and charging

A glance when you plug in or unplug, and a heads-up at 20% and 10%.

Bluetooth and AirPods Planned

See your AirPods connect, with their battery level.

Timers

Start a countdown from the notch and watch it tick beside it. A glow and a chime when time's up.

Calendar and reminders Planned

What's next today, one hover away.

File shelf Planned

Drop files on the notch to keep them handy, or send them with AirDrop.

Safe to open. Private by design.

Why macOS warns you the first time

Ember is free and made independently, so it isn't notarized by Apple (that needs a paid developer account). macOS will say it can't verify the developer. That's expected, and the steps to open it will be right here. Ember's code is public, so anyone can check exactly what it does.

Why it asks for permissions

Some features need macOS's permission: Calendar and Reminders to show what's next, and Bluetooth to show your AirPods' battery. Ember asks for each permission only when you turn on the feature that needs it, during setup or later in Settings. Say no, and only that feature stays off. You can change your mind any time in System Settings → Privacy & Security.

What stays on your Mac: everything

No account, no analytics, no tracking, no servers. Your calendar, devices and files are read on your Mac, shown in the notch, and never sent anywhere. Ember only goes online to check GitHub for updates.

What you need

  • A MacBook with a notch
  • macOS 15 Sequoia or later
  • Nothing else: free and open source (GPL-3.0)
